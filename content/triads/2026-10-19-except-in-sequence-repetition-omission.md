*Triad 26 of 32 · 2026-10-19 · Difficulty 4 · Shared form or method, Genealogy/influence, Conflict*

## The thread

Three books whose authors say they wrote none of it: Kraus's Preface ('the most lurid fantasies are quotations'), Bäcker's notes ('Every part of transcript is a quotation'), Dillard's Author's Note ('I did not write a word of it'). Each concedes the same exceptions, order and cut: Bäcker's 'except in sequence, repetition, omission'; Dillard's 'Sometimes I dropped extra words; I never added a word'. Authorship moves into that parenthesis. They part on what the cut does to the source's intention: Kraus keeps it and turns it on the speaker (with the Grumbler as commentator); Bäcker keeps it and lets the isolated document convict itself, with no commentator; Dillard discards it ('the original authors' intentions were usually first to go') and adds delight. Bäcker's own note 16 sets Himmler's 'being emigrated' beside a participle quoted by Kraus, which is most likely the Grumbler's 'compulsorily enlisted' (II, 10: 'Universal conscription has turned mankind into a passive noun'). Dillard is the control case: same method, opposite effect.

## The three books

- [[books/6351-the-last-days-of-mankind-the-complete-text|The Last Days of Mankind: The Complete Text]], [[people/karl-kraus|Karl Kraus]], [[people/fred-bridgham|Fred Bridgham]] (translator), [[people/edward-timms|Edward Timms]] (translator); 1922. *Ancestor: the documentary method with a commentator (the Grumbler); source of the participle Bäcker's note cites.*
- [[books/9312-transcript|Transcript]], [[people/heimrad-backer|Heimrad Bäcker]], [[people/patrick-greaney|Patrick Greaney]] (translator), [[people/vincent-kling|Vincent Kling]] (translator), [[people/friedrich-achleitner|Friedrich Achleitner]] (editor); 1986. *Center: the documentary method with no commentator; cites Kraus in its own notes.*
- [[books/580-mornings-like-this|Mornings Like This]], [[people/annie-dillard|Annie Dillard]]; 1995. *Control case: same method (omission, never addition) producing delight instead of indictment.*

## Notebook

1. The agentless participle: from Kraus II,10 and Bäcker p.16 with note 16, collect five present-day administrative sentences where a person is the object of a passive/participial construction; set each alone on a page; write the Grumbler's one-line comment for one; which page indicts more?
2. Omission only: build a poem from a manual by Dillard's rule (drop, never add); then isolate one sentence from the same source in Bäcker's manner; name the exact cut where delight entered, and whether the isolated version can exclude it.
3. Rotation as the smallest act: Kraus's Przemysl pair (II,16 / III,22) and Bäcker's p.38 alter only order; write a text twice, the second rotated or reversed with no word changed; two sentences on whether that is authorship by Bäcker's parenthesis.
