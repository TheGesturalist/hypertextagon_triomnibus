*Triad 24 of 32 · 2026-10-17 · Difficulty 4 · Shared form or method, Shared figure or motif, Conflict*

## The thread

Three attempts to make a work of nothing, each of which ends up describing a reader who fills it in. Lem's A Perfect Vacuum (1971) reviews Solange Marriot's nonexistent Rien du tout, a novel of pure negation: 'a critique of a book that not only does not exist but also cannot.' Dworkin's No Medium (2013) reads real blank books, erasures and silent records, and closes with 'Further Listening,' a discography of silences written in record-review voice. Cloninger's Some Ways of Making Nothing (2021) sorts 'flavors of qualitative nothing' and calls making nothing a braking of becoming, as opposed to abstaining. Exact links: the blank turns erotic in the reader (Marriot's railway station / Cocteau's Nudisme); and a three-way quarrel over whether nothing can be completed. Lem's language 'suicidally disowns itself'; Cloninger's apophasis allows no last word; Dworkin: 'there are no real absences, only replacements.'

## The three books

- [[books/7480-a-perfect-vacuum|A Perfect Vacuum]], [[people/stanisaw-lem|Stanisław Lem]], [[people/michael-kandel|Michael Kandel]] (translator); 1971. *Anchor: the impossible blank book, reviewed; the extreme position (nothing reached only by language's self-annihilation)*
- [[books/4416-no-medium|No Medium]], [[people/craig-dworkin|Craig Dworkin]]; 2013. *Empirical counterweight: real blank works read closely; denies any true blank*
- [[books/7214-some-ways-of-making-nothing-apophatic-apparatuses|Some Ways of Making Nothing: Apophatic Apparatuses in Contemporary Art]], [[people/curt-cloninger|Curt Cloninger]]; 2021. *Theory: a taxonomy of nothings and an ethics of making them; argues with both Lem (no permanent nothing) and Dworkin (nothing can be made, as braking)*

## Notebook

1. The erotics of the void. Lem's reader supplies the station and the tryst (p. 75); Nudisme offers nudity and withholds it (Dworkin, ch. 1). Write ten all-negating sentences ('The train did not arrive'), then record what you supplied reading them back. Is desire the default content of any blank, or only of blanks signed by a woman, as Lem's critic twice insinuates (p. 77)?
2. Ceasing vs. braking. 'The novel does not end: it ceases' (Lem, p. 79); 'Neither kataphasis nor apophasis ever get the last word' (Cloninger, p. 143). Write two endings for one Gestext piece: one where the language gives out, one where it is braked and can restart. Which would Cloninger call ethical (p. 421)?
3. Three reviews. Dworkin reviews a real silent record ('18 seconds too slow,' ch. 8); Lem's critic reviews an impossible book. Write three short reviews: an existing blank work, a book that cannot exist, a book of yours of which only the title exists.
