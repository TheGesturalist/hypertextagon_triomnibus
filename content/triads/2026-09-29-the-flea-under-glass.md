## The thread

Three answers to Hooke's Micrographia (1665), which is not in the library and sits in the middle as a gap; each refuses the lens differently. Cavendish (Observations upon Experimental Philosophy, 1666): the glass deludes; 'a natural Eye' and 'regular Reason' will do, and 'no Microscope is able to give us a true information' of inward actions. Swift (1726): abolish the instrument by resizing the observer; in Brobdingnag Gulliver sees lice 'with my naked Eye, much better than those of an European Louse through a Microscope', gets nausea rather than knowledge, and becomes the lens himself ('whose large Opticks were not so acute as mine'). Leskov ('The Steel Flea', 1881): the craftsman surpasses the instrument ('we just sharpened our eyes'); Lefty's nails are 'too small for any nitroscope', so the finest work is invisible and unsigned, and it stops the flea dancing. Conflict: deceives / disgusts / surpassed. Structural: in each book the unaided eye wins and pays for it.

## The three books

- [[books/349-observations-upon-experimental-philosophy-extract|Observations upon Experimental Philosophy (extract: 'sky' sections)]], [[people/margaret-cavendish|Margaret Cavendish]]; 1666 (2nd ed. 1668); file is a 15-page bilingual éditions Clinamen extract. *The philosopher's refusal: the glass deludes; natural eye plus regular reason.*
- [[books/7513-gulliver-s-travels|Gulliver's Travels]], [[people/jonathan-swift|Jonathan Swift]], [[people/ian-higgins|Ian Higgins]] (contributor); 1726. *The satirist's answer: abolish the lens by resizing the observer; Gulliver becomes the instrument.*
- [[books/2800-lady-macbeth-of-mtsensk-selected-stories-of-nikola|Lady Macbeth of Mtsensk: Selected Stories of Nikolai Leskov ('The Steel Flea')]], [[people/nikolai-leskov|Nikolai Leskov]], [[people/william-edgerton|William Edgerton]] (translator); 1881 ('The Steel Flea'); Edgerton trans. 1969; NYRB selection. *The craftsman's answer: skill surpasses the instrument, invisibly and unsigned, and the improvement stops the flea dancing.*

## Notebook

1. Proportion of distance: Cavendish, sight 'requires a certain proportion of distance betwixt the Eye and the Object' (p. 8); Swift's Lilliputian friend, fair from the ground, 'a very shocking Sight' in the hand (II.1). Describe one face or one page of your handwriting at three distances; at each, what reason adds that sight cannot.
2. The unsigned nails: Lefty's is the only shoe without a name, his nails 'too small for any nitroscope' (§14). Write on work below the threshold of attribution (commonplace entry, edit, unseen gesture), against 'machines have evened up the inequalities in gifts and talents' (§19) and Benjamin's 'The Storyteller'.
3. The shod flea won't dance: 'calculated for the most accurate exactness, and it can't carry the flea-shoes' (§15); Cavendish, 'Man ... spoils Nature with doting too much upon Art' (p. 4). Improve a finished text until it stops moving, then write the workmen's note on the cost.
