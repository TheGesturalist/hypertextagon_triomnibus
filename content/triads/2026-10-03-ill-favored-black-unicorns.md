## The thread

Three books on what a mind does when an animal arrives before its category, or a category arrives without its animal. Physiologus (Greek, perhaps 2nd c. AD; trans. Curley 1979): the unicorn as a 'nature' with no body, 'a small animal like the kid', caught in a virgin's lap, one-horned 'because the Savior said, "I and the Father are one"'; Curley's note: 'The legend of the unicorn arises out of reports concerning the rhinoceros.' Eco, Kant and the Platypus (1997): Polo meets that rhinoceros on Java, calls it a unicorn, and denies the virgin's-lap legend ('He has modified the intension and left the extension unchanged', p. 58); then the platypus's eighty-odd years of negotiation, 1797–1884. Gombrich, 'Truth and the Stereotype' (Art and Illusion, 1960): Dürer's rhinoceros, armoured from 'the dragon with its armoured body', copied into natural histories until the 18th century; copying 'proceeds through the rhythms of schema and correction'. Agreement: Gombrich and Eco both start from Kant's schematism (Gombrich's epigraph; Eco ch. 2), and Eco cites 'the classic example in Gombrich' (p. 348). Genealogy: the Physiologus is the book Polo carried to Java. They disagree about what a type is for: meaning (Physiologus), making (Gombrich), negotiation (Eco).

## Notebook

1. Write the platypus into the Physiologus: use the unicorn chapter's form (verse, 'has this nature', allegory, p. 51) for 'On the Platypus'; then write it under 'Shaw's decision of 1799 … for the moment let us describe what can be seen' (Eco p. 250). Which keeps more of the animal? Which keeps the beak?
2. Polo or Saint-Hilaire for one of your terms (gesture, qualia, labyrinth) that has met a case it doesn't fit: one entry keeps the word and corrects the description ('modified the intension and left the extension unchanged', p. 58); one holds the genus open as 'a type that must stand as a stimulus to conjecture', 'guides to action' (p. 246).
3. Drawing from hearsay, with gestures: Dürer filled secondhand evidence with the dragon (Gombrich p. 103); Montezuma's messengers mimed the horse with their bodies (Eco p. 129). Have someone describe an unseen object in words and gestures; write it; then list which of your schemata filled each gap. Is a gesture a better Nuclear Content than a word?
