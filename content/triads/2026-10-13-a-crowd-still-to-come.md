*Triad 20 of 32 · 2026-10-13 · Difficulty 4 · Shared figure or motif, Conflict, Genealogy/influence*

## The thread

Who is allowed to hear the siren? Blanchot's Ulysses hears the Sirens' 'song only still to come' safely, tied to the mast, while the oarsmen have 'only a right to the pleasure of watching their leader writhe ridiculously' (Book to Come pp. 3–9). Rancière's Mallarmé makes the 'discrete siren' the emblem of a poem waiting for 'a crowd still to come' and refuses the Saint-Simonian hymn of 'Industry made religious; religion made industrial' (pp. 5, 28, 33–34). Avraamov's Baku Symphony of Sirens (7 Nov 1922) carried that hymn out with a whole city's whistles, foghorns, artillery and choirs, conducted from a tower with flags; he 'did not want spectators' (booklet PDF p. 22), yet in Moscow 1923 the melodies were 'completely unrecognizable to the masses'. Shared figure (siren as lure, poem, factory whistle) + conflict over the one and the many (mast, conductor, tower) + genealogy (Blanchot's Mallarmé → Rancière; Saint-Simon → Proletkult). Twist: Avraamov's main instrument was tuned steam whistles ('Sinfonia Gudki'); 'sirens do not exist, except in the writings of poets.'

## Notebook

1. The tower and the mast: Blanchot's Ulysses 'held firm in the midst of hearing' (p. 8); Avraamov conducts from 'a specially built tower, using signalling flags' (PDF p. 22); Rancière's conductor 'warns the crowd about adoring itself' (p. 38). The conductor as the one who hears for the others; which of the three is honest?
2. The cancelled midday cannon: '-The midday cannon has been cancelled.' (Baku instructions, PDF p. 23); sirens for 'midday and midnight' in place of church bells (PDF p. 74); Mallarmé refuses to 'alight, today already, at some ‘central station’' (Rancière p. 34). Who owns the city's noon?
3. A siren that does not exist: 'sirens do not exist, except in the writings of poets' (Rancière p. 6); 'Avraamov himself refers to the work as Sinfonia Gudki' (PDF p. 74); 'the sound-memory of a recording that was never made' (PDF p. 62). Write the siren that exists only in translation, reconstruction and poem.
