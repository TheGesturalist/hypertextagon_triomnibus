*Triad 18 of 32 · 2026-10-11 · Difficulty 3 · Same subject, Conflict, Genealogy/influence*

## The thread

Three books whose only archive is other people's mouths. Kapuściński (Travels with Herodotus) takes Herodotus as the first reporter and catalogues his hedges ('If this is true, I don't know. I write only that which is said', ch. 18). Portelli (They Say in Harlan County; title from Florence Reece's 'They say in Harlan County') sets the oral historian's rules: 'never putting into people's mouths words they did not actually say', and 'What people believe and what they forget is as significant as what people recall accurately.' Mitchell (Up in the Old Hotel) gives the negative case: Joe Gould's 'Oral History' of 'things I heard people say' that Mitchell concludes 'doesn't exist' (Lepore 2016 disputes this), plus Mitchell's own composite Mr. Flood, 'truthful rather than factual', and his own unwritten novel. Same subject (hearsay as evidence), with conflict between Portelli's rule and Mitchell's composites, and a genealogy (Herodotus → reportage; Yeats → Gould). The question also lands on Kapuściński after Domosławski's biography.

## Notebook

1. Composite vs. bracket: Mitchell's Mr. Flood note ('truthful rather than factual') beside Portelli's 'never putting into people's mouths words they did not actually say' and his 'cutting-room' liberties. Write the rule Mitchell was following; differs in kind or degree?
2. Write in hedges: tell a contested family event using only Herodotus's formulas from Kapuściński ch. 18; then reread Portelli on belief and forgetting as evidence. What did the hedges let you keep?
3. The unwritten book as a genre: Gould's Oral History (theory, no text) and Mitchell's own novel ('I never actually wrote a word of it'). Write the entry, theory first, for a book you've described aloud but not written.
