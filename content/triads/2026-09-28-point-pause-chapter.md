*Triad 5 of 32 · 2026-09-28 · Difficulty 2 · Same subject, Genealogy/influence, Shared figure or motif, Conflict*

## The thread

Three books on how continuous language gets cut, at three scales. Parkes: the pause, punctuation as signs for a reader who has lost the speaker ('Its primary function is to resolve structural uncertainties in a text'). Szendy: the point (stigmē, from stizein, to prick, bruise) as the blow that makes experience ('It is force; it is power; it is political decision'). Dames: the chapter as a technology of time ('Our comprehension of time is continually and inescapably punctuated and divided'). Genealogy: Szendy and Dames both cite Parkes, and ¶ is C for capitulum, so the chapter's mark shrank into the paragraph sign while the chapter grew. Shared case: Tristram Shandy (Parkes's elocutionary dashes; Szendy's sash-window cut, 'the button point is unbuttoned'; Dames's chapter upon chapters on the stairs, 'a continuing motion or gesture caught midway'). Conflict: for Parkes the mark serves comprehension, for Szendy it constitutes the subject, and Dames puts the break in lived time.

## The three books

- [[books/13526-pause-and-effect-an-introduction-to-the-history-of|Pause and Effect: An Introduction to the History of Punctuation in the West]], [[people/m-b-parkes|M. B. Parkes]]; 1992 (UC Press ed. 1993). *The pause: the history, and the baseline claim that punctuation serves the reader. Source of the ¶ = C for capitulum link.*
- [[books/2071-of-stigmatology-punctuation-as-experience|Of Stigmatology: Punctuation as Experience]], [[people/peter-szendy|Peter Szendy]], [[people/jan-plug|Jan Plug]] (translator); 2013 (À coups de points); Eng. 2018. *The point: punctuation as the blow that makes experience and exercises power; the long reading of Tristram's cut.*
- [[books/11289-the-chapter-a-segmented-history-from-antiquity-to|The Chapter: A Segmented History From Antiquity to the Twenty-First Century]], [[people/nicholas-dames|Nicholas Dames]]; 2023. *The chapter: the largest regular cut as a technology of lived time; Sterne's staircase as the image of the break.*

## Notebook

1. Punctuate twice, sign both: strip a paragraph of your own (Parkes's Bleak House demonstration, p. 1); re-point it to 'resolve structural uncertainties' (Parkes p. 1) and as 'political decision' (Szendy p. 2); sign each 'legi et distincxi' like Asterius (Szendy p. 13). Which is still your text?
2. The landing: short chapters, each under five hundred words like Sterne's chapter upon chapters, each breaking midgesture (Dames pp. 161–63). Does the break register the gesture ('the doubling blow', Szendy p. 4) or make it?
3. The fossil in the pilcrow: ¶ is C for capitulum (Parkes p. 305) while the chapter swelled (Dames Table 3.1). Write on a sign that forgot what it stood for, or on Augustine's finger calling up a capitulum where the page marked nothing (Dames p. 63).
