*Triad 32 of 32 · 2026-10-25 · Difficulty 3 · Shared form or method, Same subject, Conflict*

## The thread

Three books that take the relation, not the thing, as what carries meaning, and test the claim by removing a piece. Perec's Preamble: 'the parts do not determine the pattern, but the pattern determines the parts'. Life A User's Manual walks a building's cross-section one room per chapter (99 chapters for 100 squares; knight's tour unstated in the text) and ends with Bartlebooth dead, holding a W-shaped piece over an X-shaped hole. Moretti turns Hamlet into a network (characters = vertices, speech acts = edges) and deletes vertices: removing Hamlet then Horatio — flat, unmotivated, less central than Claudius — severs the Ghost and Fortinbras from the play. Hage & Harary supply the formal vocabulary (cutpoint, bridge, hamiltonian, eulerian) and find it already in anthropology: New Hebrides sand drawings, one continuous line, finger never lifted, nothing traced twice, are eulerian. Conflict over mathematics: Moretti 'unfortunately lack[s]' it and works by hand; H&H: 'the mathematics really must be made explicit'; Perec makes it explicit and hides it.

## The three books

- [[books/773-life-a-user-s-manual|Life: A User's Manual]], [[people/georges-perec|Georges Perec]], [[people/david-bellos|David Bellos]] (translator); 1978 (trans. 1987; Vintage ebook). *Anchor: the graph narrated from inside — a building walked one room per chapter; the Preamble states the relational premise.*
- [[books/5420-network-theory-plot-analysis-literary-lab-pamphlet|Network Theory, Plot Analysis (Literary Lab Pamphlet 2)]], [[people/franco-moretti|Franco Moretti]]; 2011. *Experiment: turns Hamlet into a network and removes vertices; position, not essence, makes Horatio indispensable.*
- [[books/9870-structural-models-in-anthropology|Structural Models in Anthropology]], [[people/per-hage|Per Hage]], [[people/frank-harary|Frank Harary]]; 1983 (Calibre lists 1984; 2007 reissue). *Formal ground: exact definitions (cutpoint, bridge, hamiltonian, eulerian) and the claim that graph theory is already in anthropology.*

## Notebook

1. The W in the X-shaped hole: Perec ch. 99 vs Moretti p. 7 ('a function in the play, but not a motivation') — write the page where a piece exists but has nowhere to go; node or remainder?
2. Write under Deacon's rule (H&H p. 10: one line, ends where it began, nothing traced twice); then, using the chapter-path diagram (Stairs visited 12 times), name your own stairwell — the figure you must keep passing through.
3. Choose an edge rule for your commonplace book or the Lexicon (Moretti p. 3: 'an interaction, is a speech act'); remove your most central entry; find your Horatio. Cf. Perec Preamble: 'the ability to link this piece to other pieces'.
