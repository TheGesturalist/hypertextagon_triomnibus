## The thread

Leroi-Gourhan argues the first graphism was a rhythmic series of incised lines, abstract from the start, later bent by writing into a 'needle-sharp, but also needle-thin' line subordinated to speech. Ingold inherits the thesis, names its paradox, and adds the material history: the ruled line descends from the taut warp-thread of the loom; fully linearized, the line is 'no longer the trace of a gesture'. Hudson's study of Agnes Martin's Night Sea (1963) supplies the case: a six-foot grid of scored, gilded lines ruled with threads borrowed from Lenore Tawney's loom, whose 'visual tremolo' records the arm; after it, Martin linearized her own practice.

## The three books

- [[books/1003-gesture-and-speech|Gesture and Speech]], [[people/andre-leroi-gourhan|André Leroi-Gourhan]], [[people/anna-bostock-berger|Anna Bostock Berger]] (translator), [[people/randall-white|Randall White]] (contributor); 1964–65 (English 1993). *The source: graphism begins as rhythmic, abstract incision; writing linearizes it into a 'needle-sharp, but also needle-thin' line; the Jacquard loom exteriorizes the program.*
- [[books/10557-lines-a-brief-history|Lines: A Brief History]], [[people/tim-ingold|Tim Ingold]]; 2007. *The hinge: inherits Leroi-Gourhan's linearization thesis, names its paradox, and traces the ruled line back to the warp-thread of the loom.*
- [[books/13363-agnes-martin-night-sea|Agnes Martin: Night Sea]], [[people/suzanne-p-hudson|Suzanne P. Hudson]]; 2016. *The test case: a grid of scored, gilded lines ruled with threads from Tawney's loom; the 'visual tremolo' as Ingold's risk; after 1963 Martin linearizes her own practice.*

## Notebook

1. The obituary: Ingold 'Linearization ... marks not the birth but the death of the line' (p.151) vs Hudson on Martin painting 'what was already known' after Night Sea (pp.17-18) — was her mature method a deliberate linearization (Pye's workmanship of certainty)? 2. Abstraction first: Leroi-Gourhan 'graphism did not begin with naive representations of reality but with abstraction' (p.188) vs Martin's grid and 'the innocence of trees' (Hudson p.45) — the grid as return, not terminus; test against 'No meaning can be attached'. 3. Who rules the line: warp to ruled guideline (Ingold p.157), Martin's threads from Tawney's loom (Hudson p.36), Jacquard program 'external to the organs of execution' (Leroi-Gourhan p.250) — guide vs program; the ruler as sovereign (Ingold p.160).
