## The thread

What a movement does the morning after the End fails to arrive. Scholem's Sabbatai Ṣevi follows three disconfirmations (imprisonment, apostasy, death), each producing a new doctrine: the 'Tower of Strength', the holy ruse/descent into the qelippah, occultation and the cave 'full of light'. Schweitzer's Quest makes the delay of the Parousia the origin of Christianity ('the non-occurrence of the Parousia', ch. XIX) and reports Reimarus's thesis that the disciples 'stole the body of Jesus and hid it'. Nikolaidis's The Coming, set in Ulcinj where Sabbatai died, has his followers burn a library so he can come again, and states the thesis outright: 'The non-occurrence of the catastrophe was a catastrophe' (ch. 9); epigraph from Kafka, the Messiah 'will come only on the day after his arrival'. Hinges: Renan (quoted by Scholem p. 689; a chapter in Schweitzer) and Scholem's own 'Readers of Paul's letters can learn a great deal from these later Jewish writings' (p. 803). Conflict: delay de-eschatologizes (Schweitzer), disconfirmation radicalizes into the paradox of 'a saint who sins' (Scholem), non-arrival is itself the catastrophe (Nikolaidis). The novel's Sabbatai, checked against Scholem: 9 August for the Ninth of Ab, 30 September for 17 September 1676.

Montgomery]] (translator); 1906. *The theologian's case and genealogical middle term: Christianity as the history of the delay of the Parousia; Reimarus's stolen body; Renan's novelistic Life.*
- [[books/7415-the-coming|The Coming]], [[people/andrej-nikolaidis|Andrej Nikolaidis]], [[people/will-firth|Will Firth]] (translator); 2009. *The novelist's case: a noir in Ulcinj where Sabbatai's followers burn books so he can return; the non-occurrence of the End as the catastrophe.*

## Notebook

1. The suffering saint and the sinning saint: Scholem p. 691 ('not that of a saint who suffers … but of a saint who sins') vs Schweitzer's wheel ('That is His victory and His reign', ch. XIX); Sabbatai on the beach ('How long will you hold fast to me?' p. 917) as a third kind, the messiah who sends his followers home; write that figure's theology in ten sentences.
2. Writing the Life: Schweitzer ch. I ('no historical task which so reveals a man's true self as the writing of a Life of Jesus'), Nikolaidis ch. 1 ('I am a storyteller'), Nathan via Scholem p. 925 ('even by telling stories only' = studying the merkabah); write 500 words on a failed prophet, then list what of yourself got in.
3. Three empty places: Reimarus's stolen body (fraud), the cave 'full of light' (occultation), the Book 'dug out of its tomb' (resurrection by theft); write the fourth reading, Kafka's 'he will come only on the day after his arrival'. What is in the tomb on that day?
