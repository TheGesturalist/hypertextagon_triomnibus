## The thread

Three stories in which a man from the city or from Europe takes a wound to the head and then goes into the Argentine plain, which he already knows as a picture. Borges, 'The South' (1953): Dahlmann's forehead is brushed in the dark ('a bat? a bird?'), septicemia follows, and he takes the train to a South he knows through 'nostalgic, literary knowledge'; an old gaucho throws him a dagger. Aira, An Episode in the Life of a Landscape Painter (2000): Rugendas, Humboldt's physiognomic painter, is struck twice by lightning, survives on morphine with a ruined face, draws an Indian raid under a lace mantilla, and near the end 'A bat coming in the opposite direction brushed gently against his forehead', which answers Borges's question. Bolaño, 'The Insufferable Gaucho' (2003): an ex-judge leaves Buenos Aires in the 2001 collapse, weeps over the store in 'The South', finds 'no horses left here … only rabbits', and stabs a writer back in the city. Genealogy: Bolaño's named rewrite; Bolaño's preface opens the Aira volume; Chris Andrews translated both. Shared form: wound, then a plain already pictured, then a face-to-face ending.

- [[books/8201-an-episode-in-the-life-of-a-landscape-painter|An Episode in the Life of a Landscape Painter]], [[people/cesar-aira|César Aira]], [[people/chris-andrews|Chris Andrews]] (translator), [[people/roberto-bolano|Roberto Bolaño]] (contributor); 2000 (Un episodio en la vida del pintor viajero); Eng. 2006. *The oblique middle term: Borges's literary knowledge becomes Humboldt's procedure; the cut becomes a face ruined by lightning; the bat answers Borges's question; the face-to-face Dahlmann only steps toward.*
- [[books/14390-the-collected-short-stories-of-roberto-bolano-the|The Collected Short Stories of Roberto Bolaño ('The Insufferable Gaucho')]], [[people/roberto-bolano|Roberto Bolaño]], [[people/chris-andrews|Chris Andrews]] (translator); 2003 (El gaucho insufrible); Eng. 2010. *The declared descendant: 'The South' rewritten in the 2001 crisis as farce and entropy; rabbits for horses and cattle; the knife fight moved back to Buenos Aires.*

## Notebook

1. The bat or the bird: Borges ('a bat? a bird?', ≈12%) vs Aira ('A bat coming in the opposite direction brushed gently against his forehead', ≈95%). Write a scene where one touch on the forehead is the whole event, once with the question and once with the answer. Which keeps the second reading alive?
2. Literary knowledge / procedure / citation: Dahlmann's 'nostalgic, literary knowledge'; Humboldt's 'system of mediations' (Aira ≈88%); Pereda's 'just as he had read in Borges'. Describe a place you know mainly through books three ways (nostalgia, a fixed list of types, quotation); where it fails, write BANG BANG (Aira ≈76%).
3. Non-compensation: Krause, 'The Indians had no need of compensation'; Rugendas's face 'expressed things he did not mean to express'; Pereda, 'I believe we are losing our memory … And just as well too.' Write a manifesto for a prose that refuses to compensate, then one paragraph of it.
