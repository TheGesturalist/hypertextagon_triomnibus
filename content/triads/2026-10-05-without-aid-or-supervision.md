*Triad 12 of 32 · 2026-10-05 · Difficulty 4 · Shared form or method, Genealogy/influence, Oblique/structural*

## The thread

Three books in which a made thing exists because it is described, and in which grammar decides whose hands made it. Roby, Technical Ekphrasis (2016): Greek and Roman machine prose; Philo makes the reader see his artillery 'even if he never builds the machine' (p. 214); passives and third-person imperatives create 'a depopulated or impersonal construction space, in which the artifact seems to come together without human intervention' (p. 205); the Helping Hand, 'not about what we can do, but about what can be done'; Hero's automata 'designed to mystify an audience' (p. 5). Roussel, Impressions of Africa (1910): technical ekphrasis of machines that exist only in prose and were generated by puns ('language itself is the sole motor and guide', Polizzotti); Bedu's river loom, 'Invisibly moved by the driving belts' (ch. VI), weaves the Flood/shipwreck and runs 'without aid or supervision' (ch. X); Louise's lensless painting machine (ch. IX); the novel is built like an automaton theatre, with the performance (I–IX) before the mechanism (X–XXVI). Levé, Works (2002): 533 works 'conceived but not brought into being' (#1), 271 of 517 parsed entries opening in the agentless passive; #418 weaves a tapestry of a homonym of Roussel, a double of doubles. Shared method (description as fabrication), genealogy (Roussel to Levé), structural (the passive voice as an ethics of agency). They disagree about what a description owes the world: buildable (Roby's authors), 'nothing real' (Roussel), building left to whoever wants it (Levé).

## Notebook

1. Three voices, one work: write three unrealized works of your own (gesture, installation, book), each three ways: Levé's agentless present passive ('A picture is painted', #29); Philo's second person (Roby p. 214); Roussel's past-tense spectator ('Invisibly moved', ch. VI). Which voice makes it 'come together before the reader's eyes' (Roby p. 199)? Which makes it yours?
2. The plank and the pediment: Hero hides his works behind a plank with a pediment (Roby p. 5); Roussel gives nine chapters of performance, then seventeen of mechanism. Write one essay's gala and its backstage as separate texts; then the paragraph beginning 'It seems to me that it is my duty to reveal this method…' (Polizzotti's Introduction).
3. A double of a double: using Levé #418, write the provenance chain of one image from your commonplace book, each link 'a double (x) of a double (y)', until you reach a non-copy or admit you can't; then write it as #316, someone known less for his buildings than for his writings on other people's.
