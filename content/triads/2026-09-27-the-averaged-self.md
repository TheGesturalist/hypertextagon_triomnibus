*Triad 4 of 32 · 2026-09-27 · Difficulty 4 · Shared figure or motif, Oblique/structural, Conflict*

## The thread

A personification is a machine that turns many particulars into one speaking body. Breen describes the machine (medieval personifications as tools of thought, sorted by the universals debate into Platonic, Aristotelian/nominalist and 'Prudentian' types; 'We shape our tools and thereafter they shape us'). Erasmus's Folly is the machine taking the floor: an abstraction that refuses definition ('a sketch which would be a mere shadow of myself when I am here before you'), mocks the 'realists, nominalists, Thomists…' whose quarrel Breen uses to classify her, and ends 'forgetting who I am'. Michaux's Plume runs it backwards: a particular 'not imposing enough or universal enough to assume outright allegorical significance' (Sieburth), whose name slides into initials (A., once 'Eache'; G.; B.), and whose author concludes 'SELF is but a point of equilibrium … An averaged "self," a crowd movement.' Traffic runs downward in Breen, sideways in Erasmus, upward in Michaux. Conflict: 'I am myself wherever I am' vs 'There is no self.'

## The three books

- [[books/13964-machines-of-the-mind-personification-in-medieval-l|Machines of the Mind: Personification in Medieval Literature]], [[people/katharine-breen|Katharine Breen]]; 2021. *The machine described: the theory of personification as a tool of thought, with a typology keyed to the universals debate that places the other two books.*
- [[books/626-praise-of-folly|Praise of Folly]], [[people/desiderius-erasmus|Desiderius Erasmus]], [[people/betty-radice|Betty Radice]] (translator); 1511. *The machine takes the floor: a personification delivering her own encomium, refusing definition, mocking the universals debate, and finally forgetting who she is.*
- [[books/5489-a-certain-plume|A Certain Plume]], [[people/henri-michaux|Henri Michaux]], [[people/richard-sieburth|Richard Sieburth]] (translator); 1930. *The machine run backwards: a particular who never becomes universal; the name thins into initials and the author declares the self an averaged persona.*

## Notebook

1. Folly's definition problem: let one of your abstractions (gesture, labyrinth, utterance) deliver a first-person encomium that refuses definition ('a mere shadow of myself when I am here before you', Folly pp. 12–13) and, like Folly, forgets who it is before the end (p. 134).
2. Write an 'Eache': Michaux's A. was first named 'Eache' (Notes). Write a prose piece whose protagonist is Every, Any or Some, under Breen's rule that nominalist personifications 'prompt readers to conceptualize' universals (p. 24).
3. The persona file: build a product-design persona sheet for John Roapes as Breen's Ford engineers built 'Antonella' (pp. 1–3), against Michaux's 'an averaged "self," a crowd movement' (Postface); mark representative vs idiosyncratic details (p. 24) and what it does when it follows 'its own internal logic' (p. 4).
