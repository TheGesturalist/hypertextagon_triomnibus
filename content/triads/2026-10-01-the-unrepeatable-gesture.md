*Triad 8 of 32 · 2026-10-01 · Difficulty 3 · Shared figure or motif, Conflict, Oblique/structural*

## The thread

Three texts about a gesture that is lost and the attempts to get it back. Kleist (1810): consciousness spoils grace; the youth at the mirror cannot repeat his own pose; grace returns only in the marionette (no consciousness) or the god (infinite consciousness), 'once perception, as it were, has traversed the infinite'. Agamben (1992): 'By the end of the nineteenth century, the Western bourgeoisie had definitely lost its gestures'; cinema 'tries at once to reclaim what it has lost and to record its loss'; gesture is neither facere nor agere but gerere, 'the exhibition of a mediality', at bottom a gag. McCarthy, Remainder (2005): a man who must relearn every movement ('No Doing without Understanding') envies De Niro's seamless gestures and hires re-enactors to stage scenes until he feels 'real'; the liver lady drops her bag on the third repetition, as Kleist's youth fails on his. Conflict: return via the infinite (Kleist), via cinema and pure means (Agamben), via endless rehearsal (McCarthy), which yields 'votive' gestures that never arrive. Agamben never cites Kleist in Means Without End; the bridge is built here.

## The three books

- [[books/4209-remainder|Remainder]], [[people/tom-mccarthy|Tom McCarthy]]; 2005 (Metronome Press); Vintage ed. 2007. *Anchor: the lost gesture staged. A man who must understand every movement before doing it hires re-enactors to make his gestures feel 'real'.*
- [[books/868-selected-prose-of-heinrich-von-kleist-on-the-theat|Selected Prose of Heinrich von Kleist ('On the Theater of Marionettes')]], [[people/heinrich-von-kleist|Heinrich von Kleist]], [[people/peter-wortsman|Peter Wortsman]] (translator); 1810 (essay); Archipelago ed. 2010. *The source of the figure: grace lost to consciousness, regained only by the puppet or the god; the youth who cannot repeat his pose.*
- [[books/13685-means-without-end-notes-on-politics-notes-on-gestu|Means Without End: Notes on Politics ('Notes on Gesture')]], [[people/giorgio-agamben|Giorgio Agamben]], [[people/vincenzo-binetti|Vincenzo Binetti]] (translator), [[people/cesare-casarino|Cesare Casarino]] (translator); 1992 (essay); Eng. 2000. *The history and the definition: the bourgeoisie lost its gestures; cinema reclaims and records the loss; gesture as gerere, pure means, gag.*

## Notebook

1. Repeat a gesture ten times: Kleist's youth 'raised it ten more times: but for naught!'; McCarthy's narrator, 'Hundreds of times. More.' (ch. 9). Write one small gesture of your own ten times, a paragraph each, no sentence reused. Where does it become an 'attitude', the soul lodged 'in his elbows'? Is the tenth Kleist's comedy or McCarthy's trance?
2. Facere / agere / gerere in your practice: Agamben, 'something is being endured and supported' (p. 57). Sort a week of writing acts under Varro's three verbs; write the manifesto paragraph the gerere column implies.
3. The gag as method: the liver lady's unscripted 'Harder and harder to lift up' (ch. 8); Agamben's gag as 'the actor's improvisation meant to compensate a loss of memory' (p. 59); Kleist's 'embarrassed gestures' of those who 'don't rightly know what they wished to say'. Write a scene that breaks and gets its best line improvised.
