**[[people/john-ruskin|John Ruskin]]** · 1849 · EPUB · Calibre ID 4017

Resurfaced in [[triads/2026-10-16-the-sheen-of-antiquity|The Sheen of Antiquity]] (2026-10-16).

## Role in the triad

Western, moral counterpart: age as the glory of a building and the 'golden stain of time'; the sharpest opposite to Tanizaki's handled sheen, a prohibition on touching.

## Why this book

'Its glory is in its Age'; restoration 'a Lie from beginning to end'; 'They are not ours.' The Penguin Great Ideas text elides (with '…') the picturesque / 'Parasitical Sublimity' section, the part closest to Tanizaki.

## Quotations

*Verified against the book's own text on the day of the reading.*

- "For, indeed, the greatest glory of a building is not in its stones, nor in its gold. Its glory is in its Age" (¶11)
- "it is in that golden stain of time, that we are to look for the real light, and colour, and preciousness of architecture" (¶11)
- "Do not let us talk then of restoration. The thing is a Lie from beginning to end." (¶12)
- "Watch an old building with an anxious care; guard it as best you may, and at any cost, from every influence of dilapidation." (¶12)
- "They are not ours. They belong partly to those who built them, and partly to all the generations of mankind who are to follow us." (¶13)
- "This closing paragraph of the sixth chapter is the best, I think, in the book, – and the vainest. [1880.]" (note)
