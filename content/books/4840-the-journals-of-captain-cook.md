**[[people/james-cook|James Cook]], [[people/philip-edwards|Philip Edwards]] (editor), [[people/j-c-beaglehole|J. C. Beaglehole]] (editor)** · 1768–1779 (written); Penguin selection 1999/2003 · EPUB · Calibre ID 4840

Resurfaced in [[triads/2026-10-07-the-log-breaks-off-at-the-morai|The Log Breaks Off at the Morai]] (2026-10-07).

## Role in the triad

Primary source / shared ground: the text that stops at the morai and stages the quarterdeck proof

## Why this book

Both anthropologists argue over this archive. Cook's own text never names Lono and breaks off on 17 Jan 1779 as he is led to the morai; on 23 Nov 1773 it records the experiment Obeyesekere reads as staged proof. Edwards's Postscript adds a third, practical reading.

## Quotations

*Verified against the book's own text on the day of the reading.*

- 'as soon as we landed Touahah took me by the hand and conducted me to a large Morai, the other gentlemen with Parea and four or five more of the Natives followed.' (Third Voyage, 17 Jan 1779, last entry)
- 'hundreds were swiming about the Ships like shoals of fish.' (17 Jan 1779)

- 'He brought them iron, and they used it to kill him.' (Edwards, Postscript)
