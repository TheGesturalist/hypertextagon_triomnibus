**[[people/yoshida-kenko|Yoshida Kenkō]], [[people/g-b-sansom|G. B. Sansom]] (translator)** · c. 1330–32 · EPUB · Calibre ID 2833

Resurfaced in [[triads/2026-10-16-the-sheen-of-antiquity|The Sheen of Antiquity]] (2026-10-16).

## Role in the triad

Ancestor and hinge: the native source of Tanizaki's taste for the worn and incomplete, and the Buddhist position from which Ruskin's anxious preservation looks like attachment.

## Why this book

§81 'an air of age', §82 'It is incompleteness that is desirable' and the palace's unfinished place, §22 regret for the past, §13/§191 lamplight; the preface's 'without order or purpose' grounds the zuihitsu/architextuality link.

## Quotations

*Verified against the book's own text on the day of the reading.*

- "To while away the idle hours, seated the livelong day before the ink-slab, by jotting down without order or purpose whatever trifling thoughts pass through my mind, verily this is a queer and crazy thing to do!" (preface)
- "Truly the beauty of life is its uncertainty." (§7)
- "In all things one looks back with regret to the past. Modern fashions appear to be growing from bad to worse." (§22)
- "What one wants are things with an air of age, not showy and expensive, but of good quality." (§81)
- "To leave a thing unfinished gives interest, and makes for lengthened life. They say that even in building the palace an unfinished place is always left." (§82)
- "Are we only to look at flowers in full bloom, at the moon when clear?" (§137)
