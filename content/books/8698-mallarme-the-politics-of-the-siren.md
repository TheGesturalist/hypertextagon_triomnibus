Resurfaced in [[triads/2026-10-13-a-crowd-still-to-come|A Crowd Still to Come]] (2026-10-13).

## Role in the triad

The refusal: the discrete siren and the crowd still to come

## Why this book

Gives the siren a politics: Mallarmé waits for 'a crowd still to come' and refuses the Saint-Simonian industrial hymn — the exact negative of Baku; reads 'Salut' and 'A la nue accablante tu' as sea fables on the question Blanchot asks of Ulysses.

## Quotations

*Verified against the book's own text on the day of the reading.*

- “sirens do not exist, except in the writings of poets.” (p. 6)
- “the poet is unable to make himself heard to a crowd still to come” (p. 5)
- “it is the act, the suspension itself of fiction. It is the transformation of the narrative into a vanishing hypothesis.” (p. 7)
- “Industry made religious; religion made industrial – in order to establish the new hymn and theatre and replace the representative political machine.” (p. 28)
- “the conditions do not yet exist for the union of poet and crowd in ‘the hymn of spiritual hearts’.” (p. 33)
- “So it is pointless to try to cut short the ‘tunnel of the times’ and alight, today already, at some ‘central station’” (p. 34)
- “The orchestra conductor’s movement retains the mystery in its place and warns the crowd about adoring itself in the new temple.” (p. 38)
