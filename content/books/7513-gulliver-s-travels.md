Resurfaced in [[triads/2026-09-29-the-flea-under-glass|The Flea Under Glass]] (2026-09-29).

## Role in the triad

The satirist's answer: abolish the lens by resizing the observer; Gulliver becomes the instrument.

## Why this book

Brobdingnag turns Micrographia into lived experience (skin with 'great Holes', lice with snouts); Laputa keeps the instruments and loses the world. The edition's note names Hooke's fleas and flies as the target, so the triad's absent centre is named inside one of its books.

## Quotations

*Verified against the book's own text on the day of the reading.*

- “I could see distinctly the Limbs of these Vermin with my naked Eye, much better than those of an European Louse through a Microscope; and their Snouts with which they rooted like Swine.” (II.4)
- “I should have been curious enough to dissect one of them, if I had proper Instruments (which I unluckily left behind me in the Ship)” (II.4)
- “whose large Opticks were not so acute as mine in viewing smaller Objects” (II.3)
- “the Stumps whereof they plainly discovered through a Magnifying-Glass” (II.3)
- “although their largest Telescopes do not exceed three Feet, they magnify much more than those of a Hundred with us, and shew the Stars with greater Clearness.” (III.3)
- “So that I really began to imagine my self dwindled many Degrees below my usual Size.” (II.3)
- Editor's note: “unpleasant picture of enormously magnified insects was possibly intended as a satire on such members of the Royal Society as Robert Hooke, whose Micrographia (1665) contained some striking micrographical illustrations of fleas and flies” (Explanatory Notes, II.3)
- Excerpt: II.1, the skins passage
