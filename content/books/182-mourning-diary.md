**[[people/roland-barthes|Roland Barthes]], [[people/nathalie-leger|Nathalie Léger]] (editor), [[people/richard-howard|Richard Howard]] (translator)** · 1977-79 (written); 2009 (Journal de deuil) · EPUB · Calibre ID 182

Resurfaced in [[triads/2026-10-10-the-dated-slip|The Dated Slip]] (2026-10-10).

## Role in the triad

The vigil: dated slips with no hypothesis; the same instrument as Nabokov's turned to grief, with time's discontinuity suffered rather than sought.

## Why this book

Quartered typing paper, one note per slip; Barthes names mourning's 'Units' and 'discontinuous character'; entries silt up after the anniversary; Léger had to correct the order and calls it 'the hypothesis of a book'.

## Quotations

*Verified against the book's own text on the day of the reading.*

- "He wrote in ink, sometimes in pencil, on slips of paper (regular typing-paper cut into quarters) of which he kept a constant supply on his desk." (Léger, Foreword)
- "Who knows? Maybe something valuable in these notes?" (Oct 27, 1977)
- "In taking these notes, I’m trusting myself to the banality that is in me." (Oct 29, 1977)
- "What affects me most powerfully: mourning in layers—a kind of sclerosis. / [Which means: no depth. Layers of surface—or rather, each layer: a totality. Units]" (Nov 1, 1977)
- "What I find utterly terrifying is mourning’s discontinuous character." (Nov 26, 1977)
- "These journal notes grow rarer. Silting up. So forgetting is inexorable?" (Nov 4, 1978)
- "Each time I dream about her (and I dream only of her), it is in order to see her, believe her to be alive, but other, separate." (Mar 18, 1979)
