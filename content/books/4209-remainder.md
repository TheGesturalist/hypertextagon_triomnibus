Resurfaced in [[triads/2026-10-01-the-unrepeatable-gesture|The Unrepeatable Gesture]] (2026-10-01).

## Role in the triad

Anchor: the lost gesture staged. A man who must understand every movement before doing it hires re-enactors to make his gestures feel 'real'.

## Why this book

Makes Kleist's fallen state literal (rerouted motor functions, 'No Doing without Understanding') and takes a film actor as its model of the unfallen body, which is Agamben's cinema. The re-enactments are a puppet theatre run by a machinist; the liver lady fails on the third repetition like Kleist's youth; the novel ends weightless, turning back and forth in a plane.
