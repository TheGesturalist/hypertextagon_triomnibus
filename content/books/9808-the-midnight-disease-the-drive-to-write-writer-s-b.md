## Role in the triad

Clinical diagnosis: block as disease, defined by the suffering clause

## Why this book

Gestext. Gives the triad its instrument, a two-trait definition of block that decides whether Bartlebys are patients. Uses Kafka as a block case through mixed channels (Diaries, Felice letters via Leader, an uncited line closest to Janouch). The author is her own hypergraphic case.

## Quotations

*Verified against the book's own text on the day of the reading.*

- 'Hypergraphia is neither painful nor common. Writer’s block is both.' (Introduction)
- 'all blocked writers share two traits: they do not write despite being intellectually capable of doing so, and they suffer because they are not writing.' (ch. 3)
- 'Writers can even be hypergraphic and blocked at the same time' (ch. 3)
- 'Melville’s hypergraphia is only a fragment of his literary inspiration, reflecting his drive but not his talent.' (ch. 2)
- 'Could we get the long-awaited sequel to The Catcher in the Rye if J. D. Salinger were on Paxil?' (ch. 4)
- 'I write in order to shut my eyes.' (ch. 6, attributed to Kafka)
