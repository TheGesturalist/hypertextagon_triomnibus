## Role in the triad

Theory: the written machine

## Why this book

Supplies the vocabulary (enargeia, generativity, the Helping Hand) and the ancient precedent for machine prose that may never be built and is often written for an audience; turns Roussel and Levé from eccentrics into the late end of an old genre.

## Quotations

*Verified against the book's own text on the day of the reading.*

- “ekphrastic descriptions carry the power of enargeia, the clarity of imaginary vision sparked in the reader” (p. 2)
- “The object may be real, theoretical, or imaginary; contemporary or historical; it may be familiar or something neither author nor reader has ever experienced.” (p. 14)
- “Because automata are designed to mystify an audience, Hero takes into account the perspective of the audience as well as the designer.” (p. 5)
- “once that part of the text is done, so is the machine.” (p. 199)
- “The alternative to this ‘lived-in’ space is a depopulated or impersonal construction space, in which the artifact seems to come together without human intervention.” (p. 205)
- “while there is indeed a Helping Hand in Greek mathematics, there seems not to be a Helping Imagination.” (p. 205, quoting Creese)
